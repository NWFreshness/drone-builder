Flying controls

The two sticks work exactly like a real radio in Mode 2: the left stick is throttle (up/down) and yaw (left/right); the right stick is pitch (up/down) and roll (left/right).

Radio transmitter or gamepad (best)

Plug a radio (EdgeTX/OpenTX in USB Joystick mode) or a gamepad in and move a stick. Radios are detected as AETR (roll, pitch, throttle, yaw) with the arm switch on channel 5; check or change the mapping in Settings. Practising on your real radio is what makes the skill transfer.

Touch / mouse

Drag the two stick pads in the bottom corners. In Acro and Angle the throttle stays where you leave it, like a real radio; in GPS modes it springs back to the middle (hold height).

Keyboard

  • W/S throttle (stays put in Acro/Angle)   A/D yaw
  • ↑/↓ pitch   ←/→ roll   (or IKJL)
  • Enter arm / disarm   R reset (fresh battery)   V camera view   M flight mode   P pause

Flight modes

  • Acro (rate): what FPV pilots fly. The sticks set how fast it rotates; centre the stick and it holds whatever angle it is at. Nothing levels it for you.
  • Angle: the sticks set the tilt angle and it levels itself when you let go. A good first step.
  • GPS / Sport / Cine: camera drones. Sticks set speed; let go and it brakes and holds its spot and height.

Like the real thing

To arm, the throttle must be at the bottom and the drone level (Betaflight refuses otherwise). Props spin at idle once armed. Crashes happen above a real impact speed; R puts on a fresh battery.

Target range leaderboard

Fly to the bullseye at the far end of the range and hit it. The clock starts when you take off and stops when you hit the target, so the score is the flying time. Only the bullseye counts.

How it stays in the air

Thrust. Each prop pushes air down and the drone up. When the total thrust equals the weight it hovers; more and it climbs. The throttle stick sets this total.

Differential thrust. To tilt, the flight controller makes some motors push harder than others. Rear motors faster: the nose dips and the drone flies forward. Left motors faster: it rolls right. The drone moves by leaning its thrust, like a helicopter.

Torque cancellation. Spinning a prop twists the frame the opposite way. Half the props spin clockwise and half counter-clockwise, so at a hover the twists cancel. To yaw, the FC speeds up one set and slows the other: total thrust stays the same but the twists no longer cancel, so the frame turns.

Mixing. Every motor gets: throttle + pitch share + roll share + yaw share. The panel at the bottom shows it live: each circle is a motor, its fill is how hard it is working, its ring colour is its spin direction (red clockwise, blue counter-clockwise from above).

Collective. A helicopter has one wing and it is spinning. Collective is the angle of both blades at once: more pitch bites more air, so the same rotor speed lifts more weight. The throttle stick (or the collective lever) sets it, and the panel at the bottom shows it as one bar with the hover point marked.

Cyclic. The stick does not change the rotor speed. It tilts the whole disc: the blade pitch rises on one side of the circle and falls on the other, so the thrust vector leans and the machine accelerates that way. Return the stick and the disc comes back level, and it coasts to a stop on its own.

Anti-torque. The engine turns the main rotor one way and the fuselage tries to turn the other. The tail rotor pushes sideways to stop it. Push the yaw stick and the tail rotor changes thrust: the nose swings the other way. At a hover the machine needs a little pedal all the time, just like the real one.

Why it feels heavy. All 25 kg hangs from one mast, and the rotor has to spin up before it can save you. Drop collective and it sinks; there is no second motor to catch it. That lag, and the fact that control authority comes from the collective, is what makes a helicopter different to fly.

The gyro loop. The flight controller measures rotation thousands of times a second and keeps correcting the motors (the PID loop). Motors cannot change speed instantly: big props on a heavy drone react slowly, a 5-inch reacts in hundredths of a second. You feel that difference here.
